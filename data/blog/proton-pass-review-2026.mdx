## Security Architecture

Security is where Proton Pass genuinely shines. The entire platform is built upon a rigid, verifiable, zero-knowledge architecture.

### Zero-Knowledge Architecture

Proton Pass is built so that Proton itself cannot read your data. Encryption and decryption happen locally on your device. When your data syncs to the cloud, it is already a scrambled ciphertext. If Proton's servers were ever breached, the attackers would only find useless, encrypted data.

### AES-256-GCM & Client-Side Encryption

Proton Pass utilizes end-to-end encryption (E2EE) powered by 256-bit AES-GCM, the gold standard for symmetric encryption. Importantly, this encryption covers not just your passwords, but _all_ metadata.

### Metadata Encryption

Unlike some older password managers that leave URLs or usernames unencrypted, Proton Pass encrypts everything. An attacker cannot even see _which_ websites you have accounts for, providing total privacy regarding your browsing and usage habits.

### Key Derivation

Your master password is never sent to the server. Instead, Proton Pass uses bcrypt (a computationally expensive hashing algorithm) for strong key derivation, protecting your master password against offline brute-force attacks.

### Independent Audits & Open-Source Verification

Security through obscurity is a fallacy. All Proton Pass client applications are entirely open-source. Anyone can inspect the code. Furthermore, Proton regularly commissions independent security audits by respected third-party firms such as [Cure53](https://proton.me/), publishing the results for public scrutiny.

### Swiss Jurisdiction & Threat Model

Because Proton is headquartered in Switzerland, your data is protected by some of the world's strongest privacy laws (like the [Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection](https://proton.me/legal/privacy)). They are entirely outside of US and EU jurisdiction, and they cannot be compelled to build backdoors or hand over data without a binding order from the Swiss Federal Supreme Court.

## FAQ

### Is Proton Pass actually free?

Yes. Unlike competitors that limit you to a single device type, the free tier allows you to store an unlimited number of passwords and access them across an unlimited number of desktop and mobile devices. It is genuinely one of the most generous free plans on the market.

### Can Proton or government agencies read my passwords?

No. Proton Pass uses a strict zero-knowledge, end-to-end encryption model. Your data is encrypted locally on your device before it is ever synced to the cloud. Because Proton has no technical ability to access your decryption keys, they cannot view your vault contents or turn them over to authorities.

### Does Proton Pass support passkeys?

Yes, Proton Pass fully supports the creation, storage, and cross-platform synchronization of modern passkeys, effectively protecting you from phishing and credential stuffing attacks across Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android.

### How do the hide-my-email aliases actually work?

When registering for a new website, Proton Pass can instantly generate a random email address. Emails sent to this alias are securely forwarded to your real inbox. This protects your actual email address from spam, tracking, and data breaches. If a breach occurs, you simply disable the alias.

### Can I easily import my passwords from another manager?

Yes. Proton Pass includes built-in, seamless import tools that accept exports from LastPass, Bitwarden, 1Password, Dashlane, Chrome, Firefox, and various other password managers, mapping your data automatically.

### Can I export my data if I want to leave?

Absolutely. Proton Pass does not believe in vendor lock-in. You can easily export your entire vault—including secure notes and TOTP seeds—into a standard, readable format at any time.

### How does account recovery work if I forget my master password?

Because of its zero-knowledge design, Proton cannot reset your master password. Upon account creation, you are provided with a recovery phrase. You must store this phrase securely offline; it is the absolute only way to recover your account if you forget your master password.

### Is Proton Pass good for business use?

It is excellent for freelancers, remote workers, and small teams who want to share vaults securely. However, large enterprises requiring advanced Active Directory syncing or strict corporate policy management should look at enterprise-specific solutions.

### Does it work on Linux?

Yes. Linux users can utilize the fully-featured browser extensions, and Proton is actively working on expanding native desktop support for various Linux distributions.

### Can I use it for my whole family?

Yes. The vault sharing feature allows you to create specific family vaults for sharing streaming passwords, bank logins, or home Wi-Fi credentials safely with encrypted access control.

### Is the mobile experience reliable?

Very reliable. The iOS and Android apps are fast, support biometric unlocking (FaceID/TouchID), and integrate deeply with native OS autofill APIs to make mobile browsing secure and frictionless.

### Do I need to use Proton Mail to use Proton Pass?

No. While it integrates beautifully with the wider Proton ecosystem, you can use Proton Pass entirely independently and forward your email aliases to any email provider, including Gmail, Outlook, or Fastmail.

### What is Proton Sentinel?

Available on premium plans, [Proton Sentinel](https://proton.me/) is an advanced security feature that uses AI and human analysis to detect and block suspicious login attempts, protecting you even if an attacker has somehow managed to obtain your master password through external means.
